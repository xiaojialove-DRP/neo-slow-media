# Productive vs. Consumptive Friction

Part of xiaojiahaina's neo-slow media framework. Not all friction is the
enemy — this is a direct challenge to mainstream "remove all friction" UX
thinking.

**Consumptive friction** drains without return: bureaucratic loops with no
resolution, complexity that serves the platform rather than the person,
confusion from poor information design, waiting without understanding why.

**Productive friction** requires something of the person and returns more in
exchange: a question that makes you stop and think before acting, a process
that builds skill through resistance, a delay that creates space for
reflection, a constraint that forces a genuine choice.

**The test**: does this friction cost the person something and give back
skill, reflection, or a genuine choice in return — or does it just cost them
something?

**Applying it**: the goal is never "remove all friction." Identify which
friction is consumptive (remove it) and which is productive (preserve or
redesign around it). A flow that eliminates *all* friction often also
eliminates the moments where the person would have grown, decided, or
noticed something. The best designs replace consumptive friction with
productive friction — they don't just make things faster.

## A limit worth naming

This test alone doesn't check whether the underlying goal is worth serving
in the first place. A friction can pass this test — cost something, return
skill or reflection — while still serving a hollow or fake goal underneath.
If the question is "should we even be building this," not just "how should
this step work," use `neo-slow-media-lens` instead, which checks that layer
too.
