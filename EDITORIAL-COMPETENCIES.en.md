# Seven Editorial Competencies

*An appendix to the [Neo-Slow Media field guide](FIELD-GUIDE.en.md), for reviewing
content and copy specifically — not product or feature concepts.*

When reviewing writing, messaging, or content strategy, these trained
editorial skills are the applicable standard:

1. **Capture the core under time pressure** — find the one thing that
   actually matters in a pile of information, fast, without going shallow.
2. **Listen first** — what someone says is rarely exactly what they mean.
   Find the story beneath the stated ask.
3. **Domain knowledge as a sensory organ** — accumulated expertise should let
   you instantly recognize signal from noise, not just look things up.
4. **Complexity in, clarity out** — the job is not to dishonestly simplify,
   but to find the structure that was always there, hidden under the noise.
5. **Narrative craft and high aesthetic standards** — form is not decoration,
   it's argument. How something is told determines whether it lands.
6. **Hold open the contradictions** — don't flatten genuine complexity into a
   single convenient point of view; forcing resolution where none exists is
   a form of dishonesty.
7. **Protect diversity of perspective** — actively resist centering the
   loudest voice; seek the underrepresented angle, the experience that
   doesn't fit the dominant narrative.

Specifically flag language or mechanics that entertain (produce a hit of
interest that evaporates) while claiming to help (imply lasting benefit) —
this gap between claimed value and actual mechanism is what Lens 2
(Entertain vs. Engage) in the main framework is built to catch.
